What is a Drawdown in Prop Trading?

By FundedEdge · 5 min read

If you're new to prop trading, "drawdown" is probably the scariest word you'll see. It appears in every firm's rulebook, and breaching it means losing your account — no matter how profitable you are.

In this guide, we'll explain drawdown in plain English, why every firm has it, and how to avoid breaching it.

What is drawdown?

Drawdown is the drop in your account balance from its highest point.

Example: If your account starts at $100,000 and drops to $95,000, your drawdown is $5,000 — or 5%.

Simple. But prop firms measure drawdown in two specific ways: daily and maximum.

Daily drawdown

Daily drawdown is the maximum you can lose in a single trading day.

Most firms set this at 5%. So if your account starts the day at $100,000, you cannot let it drop below $95,000 that day. If it does → account terminated.

Example

Your $100K account starts today at $100,000. Daily drawdown limit = 5%.
You have a losing day and your balance drops to $94,500.
→ You've breached daily drawdown. Account closed.

Some firms calculate daily drawdown from your starting balance of the day. Others calculate from your equity high of the day. Always read the firm's rules carefully — this detail matters.

Maximum drawdown

Maximum drawdown is the total amount you can lose from your starting balance across the entire challenge.

Most firms set this at 10%. If your account started at $100,000, the floor is $90,000. If you hit $90,000 at any point → account terminated.

Example

Your $100K account has a 10% max drawdown = $90,000 floor.
You have several losing days but stay above $90,000.
→ You're still in the challenge.

Static vs trailing drawdown

This is the most important detail that catches new traders out.

Static drawdown (easier)

The drawdown floor never moves. It's always the same amount below your starting balance.

Example: $100K account, 10% max drawdown → floor always at $90,000, no matter how high your profits go.

Trailing drawdown (harder)

The drawdown floor moves up as your account grows. It trails your highest balance.

Example: $100K account, 10% trailing drawdown.

  • Balance hits $105,000 → floor moves to $95,000
  • Balance hits $110,000 → floor moves to $100,000
  • Balance hits $115,000 → floor moves to $105,000

Trailing drawdown is much harder to manage. Many futures firms (Apex, Topstep) use trailing. Forex firms (FTMO, FundedNext) usually use static.

💡 Rule of thumb: Static drawdown = easier. Trailing = harder. Always check which your firm uses.

Why do firms use drawdown limits?

Simple: prop firms want traders who manage risk, not gamblers.

If you can't survive a 5% daily loss without blowing the account, you don't have a sustainable strategy. Drawdown rules force traders to:

  • Use proper position sizing
  • Set stop losses
  • Not revenge trade after losses
  • Manage risk consistently

Every professional trader lives by these rules anyway. Drawdown limits just enforce them.

How to avoid breaching drawdown

1. Risk 0.5% per trade

If you risk 0.5% per trade, you can lose 10 trades in a row before hitting a 5% daily drawdown. This gives you room to have bad days.

2. Stop for the day after 2 losses

Two losses = 1% down. Stop, review, trade again tomorrow. This prevents the "one bad day" scenario.

3. Set hard stop losses

Never enter a trade without a stop loss. Ever. A single trade without a stop can wipe out your daily drawdown.

4. Check the firm's calculation method

Daily drawdown from starting balance vs equity high — this changes your risk. Read the rules.

5. Track your drawdown mentally

If you started the day at $102,000 and daily drawdown is 5%, your floor is $96,900. Know this number before you trade.

Final thoughts

Drawdown isn't your enemy — it's the rule that keeps you disciplined. Learn it, respect it, and it will actually make you a better trader.

The traders who fail challenges usually blow their drawdown, not their profit target. Protect your downside first.
